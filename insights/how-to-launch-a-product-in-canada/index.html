How to launch a product in Canada

A launch is not an announcement. Here’s a simple three-phase plan that builds demand before launch day and keeps it going after.

By Taimoor Syed  ·  November 2, 2026  ·  6 min read

Canada is a tricky market to launch in. People are spread across a huge country, attention is split across platforms, and getting in front of the right audience costs more than it used to. None of that makes launching here impossible. It just means you can’t post on launch day and hope.

Why launches fall flat

The usual pattern is to pick a date, post a few things, maybe send a press release, launch, then watch the spike fade. The attention was real, but there was nothing behind it. No audience built beforehand, no message holding it together, and no plan for the weeks after.

A launch isn’t one moment. It’s three phases: build demand, capture attention, keep the momentum.

Phase 1: Build demand (4 to 6 weeks before)

By launch day, the right people should already know you exist and understand why you matter. That means starting before you’re ready to sell.

  • Publish content about the problem your product solves, not just the product
  • Collect emails with a waitlist or early access offer
  • Run small, targeted awareness campaigns aimed at your actual audience
  • Reach out to relevant media, newsletters and industry voices

Decide early whether your launch is local, provincial or national. A launch aimed at Metro Vancouver needs different channels from one aimed at businesses across every province, and trying to do both at once usually weakens both.

Phase 2: Launch week

If the first phase worked, launch week feels like a release people were waiting for. Your job is to convert the warm audience you built and widen your reach beyond it.

One mistake to avoid is spending the whole budget this week. It creates a spike that drops straight back down. Spread your spend across all three phases.

Phase 3: Keep the momentum (4 to 8 weeks after)

This is the phase most people skip, and it’s where a lot of launches are won. Many Canadian buyers take their time on anything above a small purchase, so people who noticed you at launch often aren’t ready to buy until weeks later.

  • Share customer stories and early results
  • Keep explaining the value in different ways
  • Retarget people who engaged but didn’t buy

Give it one idea

Before any of the tactics, decide what the whole launch is about. What’s the one belief or story every post, email and ad will express? Without that, the three phases feel like three different companies. With it, people see one brand entering the market.

A short checklist

  1. Pick one goal and put a number on it
  2. Decide local, provincial or national
  3. Choose the one idea the launch is built around
  4. Plan all three phases and split your budget across them
  5. Start building your audience before you have anything to sell

Before you start, it’s also worth checking whether your marketing has a clear goal and message. A launch magnifies whatever is already there.
