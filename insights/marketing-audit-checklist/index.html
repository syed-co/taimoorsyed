A marketing audit checklist: 15 questions to ask about your own marketing

You don’t need a consultant to find the big problems. Start with these 15 questions and an afternoon.

By Taimoor Syed  ·  November 6, 2026  ·  6 min read

Most marketing problems show up in the answers to a handful of basic questions. If you can’t answer them quickly and with confidence, that’s useful information in itself.

Grab a coffee, open your analytics and your last six months of spend, and work through these. Write the honest answer, not the one you wish was true.

Goals

  1. What is the one result marketing needs to deliver in the next 90 days, and what’s the number?
  2. Does everyone involved in marketing, including agencies and freelancers, give the same answer?
  3. How are you measuring it today?

Audience and message

  1. Who is your best customer, described in one sentence?
  2. Can a stranger read your homepage and tell what you do and who it’s for within five seconds?
  3. Do your ads, emails, social posts and website all say the same core thing?

Channels and spend

  1. Where did your last 12 months of marketing spend go, in time and money?
  2. Which channel brings you the best customers, not just the most clicks?
  3. If you had to cut a third of your marketing spend tomorrow, what would you cut first, and why?

Website and conversion

  1. What do you want a visitor to do on your site, and is that action obvious on every page?
  2. Does the site load quickly on a phone?
  3. When someone fills in a form or calls, how quickly does someone respond?

Tracking and follow-through

  1. Can you tell where your last 10 customers came from?
  2. Does marketing hear back from sales about which leads were any good?
  3. Is there a regular time when someone reviews the results and changes the plan?
Every “I’m not sure” is a place where money is probably leaking.

How to read your answers

If most of your “not sure” answers sit in the first group, you have a goal problem. Fix that before anything else. If they’re in the second group, it’s a message problem. In the third, a spending problem. In the fourth, a conversion problem. In the fifth, a measurement problem.

Fix them in that order. A great ad pointing at an unclear message wastes money, and better tracking on top of a missing goal just measures the wrong thing more precisely.

If you’d rather have a second set of eyes on it, that’s what a marketing audit is for. And if the first group is where you got stuck, start with why marketing isn’t working and how to find the real problem.
