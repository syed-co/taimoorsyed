Why sales stalled, and where to look first

Before you hire, discount or panic, spend 30 minutes working out where deals are actually falling off.

By Taimoor Syed  ·  November 2, 2026  ·  5 min read

When sales flatten, the instinct is to do more of everything. More leads, more outreach, a promotion, maybe a new hire. Sometimes that’s right. A lot of the time it just pours more into a leak you haven’t found.

I’ve done my share of cold calling and door knocking, and the lesson that stuck is that sales problems are almost always specific. Something is breaking at one particular point. Your job is to find which point before you spend money.

Start by counting

Write out the steps a customer goes through, from first contact to signed deal. For most businesses it looks something like lead, first conversation, proposal, decision, close. Then pull the last three months and count how many people made it through each step.

You don’t need software for this. A spreadsheet is fine. What matters is that the numbers show you where people drop off, because each drop points to a different fix.

If there aren’t enough leads at the top

This is a visibility or targeting problem. Are you in front of the right people? Is your message clear enough that they can tell in a few seconds whether you’re for them? More activity helps here, but only if it’s aimed at the right audience.

If leads come in but conversations don’t happen

Look at speed and follow-up. How fast does someone respond? Who owns the next step? Leads go cold quickly, and a surprising number of deals are lost simply because nobody got back in touch in time.

If conversations happen but proposals don’t get signed

This is usually about the offer, the price or the fit. Pull your last 10 lost deals and your last 10 wins and read them side by side. Ask what the lost ones had in common. Common answers are that they weren’t the right size of customer, the value wasn’t clear, or a competitor made the decision feel safer.

If deals close, but slowly

Long cycles often come from too many people involved or unclear next steps. Try ending every call with a specific next step and a date. It sounds basic, and it shortens cycles more than most people expect.

Sales slumps feel personal, but they’re usually a leak in one place. Find it first.

A 30 minute check you can do today

  1. List your sales steps from first contact to close.
  2. Count how many people reached each step in the last 90 days.
  3. Find the biggest drop.
  4. Pull 10 wins and 10 losses and note what separates them.
  5. Pick one fix for that one point and give it a month before changing anything else.

Don’t forget the marketing side

If the top of the funnel is the weak spot, the issue may sit with your marketing rather than your sales process. In that case it helps to look at whether your marketing has a clear goal and message before adding spend.
