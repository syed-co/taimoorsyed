Why your marketing isn’t working (and how to find the real problem)

It’s rarely the budget, and it’s rarely the platform. Here are the three things I check first.

By Taimoor Syed  ·  November 2, 2026  ·  5 min read

When someone tells me their marketing isn’t working, the first thing I ask is what it’s supposed to be doing. Most of the time there’s a pause. That pause is usually the problem.

It’s almost never the budget. It’s not the industry or the platform either. It’s how the marketing is put together. Here are the three causes I see most often.

1. There’s no clear goal

“Get more customers” isn’t a goal. It’s a wish. A goal sounds more like “book 20 qualified conversations a month with companies our size.” That version tells you who you’re after, how many you need, and how you’ll know it worked.

Without it, you can’t judge whether a video, an ad or an email was worth the money, because you never decided what worth meant.

If you can’t say what winning looks like in the next 90 days, you’re running activity, not marketing.

2. Everything says something different

The video tells one story. The social posts tell a slightly different one. The ads send people to a website that says something else again. Each piece might look great on its own, but someone who sees three of them walks away remembering none.

The fix is boring and it works. Pick one idea and have everything repeat it in different formats. The video introduces it, the posts reinforce it, the ads go after the people who watched. One idea, seen often, sticks.

3. Nothing builds on anything else

A lot of marketing resets every month. You spend January’s budget and by February there’s nothing to show for it. Marketing that works tends to stack. The awareness you built in month one makes lead generation easier in month two. The content from month two gives your sales conversations something to point to in month three.

If you stopped spending tomorrow and everything stopped with it, that’s a sign nothing is building.

A quick way to check

Ask these four questions about what you’re doing right now:

  • Does each piece of marketing serve one specific goal?
  • Does each piece repeat the same core idea?
  • Is there a plan for how each piece feeds the next?
  • Could I explain all of it in two sentences?

If you answered no to any of them, you’ve probably found your problem.

What to do this week

  1. Write down the one result you need in the next 90 days. Put a number on it.
  2. List everything you’re spending money and time on.
  3. Cross out anything that doesn’t clearly connect to that result.
  4. Build what’s left around a single message.

You don’t need a bigger budget for this. You need a smaller, clearer plan.

When it isn’t really a marketing problem

Sometimes marketing is working fine and the leak is somewhere else. Leads come in and nobody follows up fast enough. The offer isn’t clear. The price doesn’t match what people expect. If the goal and the message are solid and results are still flat, look at the sales side next. I wrote about where to start in why sales stalled and where to look first.
